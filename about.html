Who is Lord Ganesha?

"गजाननं भूतगणादिसेवितं कपित्थजम्बूफलसारभक्षणम्"

Lord Ganesha, also known as Ganapati, Vinayaka, and Vighnaharta, is one of the most revered deities in Hinduism. He is the son of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati, and is worshipped as the god of wisdom, prosperity, and new beginnings.

With his distinctive elephant head, large ears, and pot-bellied body, Ganesha's form is deeply symbolic. He is the first deity to be invoked before any auspicious ceremony, prayer, or new venture — for he removes obstacles and grants success to his devotees.

His vahana (vehicle) is the humble mouse, and his favorite offering is the sweet modak. Ganesha is celebrated across India and the world, with Ganesh Chaturthi being his most grand and beloved festival.

The Divine Story

The legend of how Ganesha came to be — a tale of devotion, wisdom, and divine love

The Birth of Ganesha

According to Hindu mythology, Goddess Parvati created Ganesha from the sandalwood paste she used for her bath. She brought him to life and posted him as a guard at her door while she bathed, instructing him not to let anyone enter.

When Lord Shiva returned and tried to enter, Ganesha stopped him. A fierce battle ensued, and in his rage, Shiva severed Ganesha's head. When Parvati saw this, she was heartbroken and demanded Ganesha be brought back to life.

Shiva agreed and ordered his attendants to bring the head of the first creature they found — which was an elephant. Shiva placed the elephant head on Ganesha's body and brought him back to life, declaring him his son and the leader of his divine army (Ganas), thus the name "Ganapati."

Meaning of Ganpati

Ga + Na + Pati

"Ga" means wisdom, "Na" means liberation, and "Pati" means lord — the Lord of Wisdom and Liberation.

Vighnaharta

The remover of all obstacles and difficulties — the one who clears the path for success and prosperity.

Ganadhipa

The leader of the Ganas — Lord Shiva's divine attendants, representing the master of all beings.

Importance of Ganesh Chaturthi

Ganesh Chaturthi is one of the most significant festivals in Hindu culture, celebrated with immense devotion and grandeur, especially in Maharashtra. It marks the birth of Lord Ganesha and is observed for 10 days during the Hindu month of Bhadrapada.

  • Invokes wisdom, prosperity, and good fortune
  • Brings communities together in shared devotion
  • Preserves and promotes Indian cultural heritage
  • Celebrated as a symbol of environmental consciousness
  • A time for new beginnings and fresh resolutions

Symbolism of Lord Ganesha

Every aspect of Ganesha's form carries deep spiritual meaning and wisdom

Elephant Head

The elephant head represents supreme wisdom, intelligence, and discriminating power. It signifies thinking big and remaining calm under all circumstances.

Large Ears

Ganesha's large ears symbolize the importance of listening — to absorb knowledge, understand others, and be attentive to the prayers of devotees.

The Trunk

The trunk, capable of both uprooting trees and picking up a needle, represents adaptability — the power to handle both great and small tasks with equal grace.

Modak

The modak, Ganesha's favorite sweet, symbolizes the reward of spiritual pursuit — the sweet inner bliss that comes from wisdom and self-realization.

The Mouse (Mushak)

The mouse as Ganesha's vehicle represents desire — the need to keep desires small and under control, just as the small mouse carries the great Ganesha.

Large Belly

Ganesha's pot belly symbolizes the capacity to digest all experiences — good and bad — and the ability to peacefully absorb life's ups and downs.

10 Days of Ganpati Celebration

A sacred journey of devotion — from Sthapana to Visarjan, each day holds special significance

Day 1
Ganesh Chaturthi — Sthapana

The auspicious installation of Lord Ganesha's idol. Devotees welcome Bappa with the prayer "Ganpati Bappa Morya!" and perform the Pran Pratishtha ritual.

Day 2
Panchamrit Snan

The idol is bathed with panchamrit — a mixture of milk, curd, honey, ghee, and sugar — followed by offerings of flowers and modaks.

Day 3
Saptami — Devotional Songs

Bhajan sandhya and devotional singing fill the air. Communities gather for evening prayers and cultural performances.

Day 4
Ashtami — Special Puja

A special puja with 108 names of Ganesha (Ashtottara Shatanamavali) is chanted, along with offerings of 21 modaks.

Day 5
Naivedya Offerings

Special food offerings (naivedya) including modaks, laddoos, and other sweets are prepared and distributed as prasad.

Day 6
Cultural Celebrations

Grand cultural programs featuring classical music, dance performances, and community competitions are organized.

Day 7
Gauri Puja

Goddess Gauri (Parvati) is worshipped alongside Ganesha, celebrating the divine mother-son bond with special rituals.

Day 8 & 9
Community Service & Bhajans

Acts of charity, food distribution, and continuous bhajan sessions strengthen community bonds and devotion.

Day 10
Anant Chaturdashi — Visarjan

The grand farewell — Bappa is immersed in water with the emotional promise: "Pudhchya varshi lavkar ya!" (Come back soon next year!).
